The Macro Problem

The Post-Subsidy Transit Crisis in Lagos

Why private cars and commercial ride-hailing broke down simultaneously.

⚠ Petrol Price Explosion

Petrol rose from ₦195/L to over ₦1,000/L. The monthly fuel cost for a Lekki-to-VI commuter climbed from ₦35,000 to over ₦160,000. Driving alone is no longer economically viable for corporate workers.

⚠ Ride-Hailing Is Now a Luxury

Uber and Bolt fares reach ₦8,000 to ₦14,000 one-way during morning rush hour. A daily round-trip commute costs ₦320,000 to ₦500,000 monthly, exceeding the net take-home salary of most mid-level professionals.

⚠ Danfos Fail Corporate Standards

Commercial yellow buses (danfos) and kekes lack air conditioning, safety standards, and predictable schedules. Corporate workers face theft, harassment, and severe delays.

⚠ Uncoordinated Corridor Waste

450,000+ private cars sit in the same 2-hour gridlock on Lekki-Epe Expressway and Third Mainland Bridge every morning, each carrying only one occupant.

The Solution

Closed Corporate Corridor Carpooling

A structured peer transit network connecting verified professionals along primary highways.

✓ Fixed Arterial Corridor Matching

Zero residential estate detours. Drivers pick up passengers directly along primary highways at pre-approved CCTV Safe Zones (Circle Mall, TotalEnergies, Jubilee Bridge).

✓ Fair Statutory Fuel Split

Predictable ₦1,200 to ₦1,800 contribution per seat. Riders save 80% compared to Uber. Drivers earn up to ₦5,400 daily to offset their monthly petrol budget.

✓ Zero-Cash Escrow Architecture

No physical cash ever changes hands in the car. Fares are held digitally in escrow and automatically released upon one-tap curbside boarding verification.

✓ Integrated Roadside Tow Shield

Emergency dispatch network for mechanics, flatbed towing, battery boosts, and fuel delivery guarantees commuters are never stranded along expressway bottlenecks.
